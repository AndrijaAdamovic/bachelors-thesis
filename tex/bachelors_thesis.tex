\subsection*{Dynamical Systems}
Although Willems' Fundamental Lemma stems from the behavioural approach to sytems theory, in this thesis it is formulated using the 
classical state-space LTI system formulation. This formulation is sufficient for the further chapters of the thesis. 

\begin{definition} 
    A discrete-time linear time-invariant (LTI) system is a dynamical system whose dynamics are described by:
    \begin{subequations}
        \begin{align}
            x(k+1) &= Ax(k) + Bu(k) \\
            y(k) &= Cx(k) + Du(k)
        \end{align}
    \end{subequations}
    Where \(t \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}\), \(x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{n}\) is the state, \(u(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{m}\) is the input
    and \(y(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{p}\) is the output of the system. \\
    The true state space dimension is $n$ and system matrices \(A \in M_n(\mathbb{R})\), \(B \in M_{n \times m}(\mathbb{R})\), 
    \(C \in M_{p \times n}(\mathbb{R})\), \(D \in M_{p \times m}(\mathbb{R})\).
\end{definition}

In our data-based approach, those matrices (i.e. the system model) are unknown. However, an upper bound on state space dimension is
given $N \geq n$. Now lets define the system trajectories.

\begin{definition}
    A sequence of ordered triples \(\left( u(t), x(t), y(t) \right)_{t=0}^{\infty}\) is called an input-state-output trajectory
\end{definition}
